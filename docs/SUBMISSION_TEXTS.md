# SELITH AGRO — NASA Space Apps submission texts (English)

## Project Name
SELITH AGRO

## Summary (≈170 words)
SELITH AGRO is a bilingual (Spanish/English) web app that helps smallholder farmers in Tizimín, Yucatán, decide what to plant as the climate shifts. A farmer draws their parcel on a satellite map, sees real NASA and ESA observations at a 100 m grid, and asks questions by voice or text. A deterministic rotation engine compares three strategies (the current rotation plus two alternatives) and explains why, with evidence, information gaps and risks. A language model only explains the engine's result; it never decides. Every answer starts labeled "AI only" and is then reviewed by an agronomy expert of the matching area, who can confirm, correct or revoke it. Mexico has little local agronomic data, so we fuse NASA Earth observations (Landsat, MODIS, IMERG, GLDAS, ECOSTRESS, GRACE-FO, NASADEM, POWER) with FAO GAEZ crop suitability, and we measured how far global products miss ground truth using USDA data in Hidalgo County, Texas. Agronomic parameters are provisional until local experts validate them, and the app says so everywhere.

## Project Details
**What it does.** The farmer enters (simulated demo access, no email or password), draws a parcel and gets its area in square meters. The app builds a 100 m grid and loads real layers per cell: Sentinel-2 vegetation (10 m), Landsat and MODIS vegetation, MODIS surface temperature and evapotranspiration, GPM IMERG rainfall, GLDAS soil moisture, ECOSTRESS temperature, GRACE-FO water storage and NASADEM slope. From these it computes a transparent vulnerability index. In the chat, a deterministic dynamic-programming engine ranks crop rotations under three hard constraints (water, plant health, market) and a Monte Carlo run gives P10/P50/P90 ranges. FAO GAEZ adds suitability and yield today and under future climate (maize yield at this site falls about 40% under RCP 8.5, 2041–2070).

**Human in the loop.** The farmer always decides. Answers are labeled "AI only, no academic backing" until an expert of the right area (assigned at random within the area) confirms, corrects or revokes them; there is a "Reviewed reports" view.

**Honest uncertainty.** We tested Sentinel-2 NDVI against ~1–3 m NAIP imagery (correlation 0.85) and SoilGrids against SSURGO soil measurements in Hidalgo County, Texas (texture off by 3–12%, organic matter far off). We carry that uncertainty forward. Agronomic parameters are marked provisional with their source status; synthetic data is always labeled "example". Layers not yet loaded (SMAP, GEDI) are shown as examples.

**Impact.** A tool a farmer can use by voice on a phone browser, offline-capable (PWA), that turns open Earth data into decisions while keeping experts in the loop.

**Built with.** Python (FastAPI, PostGIS, NumPy, rasterio, xarray), TypeScript/React, MapLibre GL, a deterministic engine with a TypeScript twin tested for parity (hypothesis property tests), Claude (Sonnet) for explanations, Vercel and Neon for hosting. Open source (MIT).
